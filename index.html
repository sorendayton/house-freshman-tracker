Method and caveats

Who is included

The tracker covers every district where Wikipedia's results table does not show the incumbent re-elected, plus the three delegate seats that opened or changed hands (the District of Columbia, the Virgin Islands and Guam). That means called winners, the leader and the trailer in each uncalled race, and both candidates in the two Louisiana runoffs on December 12.

Six former members return after time away: Carl (R-AL), Solis (D-CA), Bean (D-IL), Allred (D-TX), Luria (D-VA) and McAdams (D-UT). They are new to this Congress but not true freshmen, and their rows say so. Incumbents who changed districts (Democrats Casar, Wasserman Schultz and Menefee, and Republican Kennedy) are left out, as is CA-40, where two Republican incumbents ran against each other.

A trailing candidate stays on the list only while within 3 points of the leader; Vang (D, CA-7), Hernandez (D, CA-14) and Hill (I, AK-AL) were further back. Candidates come off once their race is called against them. So far that is one Republican, Green (FL-9), and six Democrats: Watson (NC-8), Nez (AZ-2), Locklin (FL-26), Rodriguez (FL-27), Conroy (WA-5) and Khalifa (SC-2).

Legislative service

Prior service and committee assignments come from each member's Ballotpedia page, checked against Wikipedia. Committees are listed with the most recent session first. Tribal and territorial legislatures count. Leadership posts come from Ballotpedia's committee listings, and each chamber-level post (floor leader, whip, president pro tem, caucus chair) was confirmed with a separate search. Where Ballotpedia has no committee record, mostly for older service such as that of Democrats Biss, Ojeda and McAdams (in McAdams's case, the Utah Senate), the row says so.

Seat lean

Each district shows its Cook Partisan Voting Index as given in Wikipedia's 2026 results tables. Several states redrew their maps mid-decade, so check a figure against Cook before quoting it.

On these numbers, 211 of the 435 seats are even or lean Democratic, and Democrats won all of them. No Republican holds a seat bluer than R+4. A majority takes 218 votes, so Democrats need at least seven Republican-leaning seats to hold the House.

They have far more than seven, and most belong to freshmen. Counting leaders in uncalled races, 49 Democrats sit in Republican-leaning seats; 38 are freshmen. In seats R+5 or redder, 22 of the 26 Democrats are freshmen.

Few of those freshmen have legislative experience. Trump carried 22 of their districts by 10 points or more in 2024, and only four of the Democrats elected there have served in a state legislature (Ford, Benitez-Thompson, Ojeda and Smith). Of the other 57 Democratic freshmen, 25 have.

Caucus predictions

Ideological caucus tags rest on four kinds of evidence, strongest first: the caucus PAC's endorsement list; earlier membership; the member's own statements (DSA membership, for example); and the district and biography. The endorsement lists are the NewDem Action Fund's (50 candidates), the Blue Dog PAC's 2026 list, the Freedom Caucus Fund, Republican Main Street PAC, the Congressional Progressive Caucus PAC and Justice Democrats.

Republicans default to the Republican Study Committee unless something points elsewhere. The RSC's executive board endorsed Ben Cline (R-VA) on September 18 to chair the caucus in the 120th Congress; the members have not yet voted (RSC).

Identity caucuses (the Black, Hispanic, Asian Pacific American and Native American caucuses, along with the Equality, veterans, labor and law-enforcement caucuses) are inferred from each member's background. They appear in grey, apart from the ideological tags, until a caucus names the member; the tag then moves into the main list as confirmed.

Confirmed and predicted

H, M and L are predictions. A tag becomes C, for confirmed, only when the caucus or the member says so publicly: a caucus release that names the member, the member's own statement, or the official roster after January 3. A PAC's list of the candidates it backed does not count, even when the PAC calls them "confirmed." Each confirmed tag keeps its earlier prediction, so the page can be scored against the outcome. Predictions that fail will be marked once memberships settle early next year.

Freshmen usually announce caucus memberships in December and January. So far the only confirmations come from CAPAC, which named five incoming members on November 4.

What the caucuses say

As of November 9:

  • The Blue Dog PAC says the coalition is "on track to more than double," with 22 members already confirmed, and lists the candidates it backed who won (Blue Dog PAC; outlook). One of them, Johnson (D, MN-1), is in a race not yet called.
  • Greg Casar (D-TX), the Progressive Caucus chair, said on November 8 that the caucus would add "at least 25 new members" but did not name them (report; American Prospect).
  • The Hispanic Caucus says "more than 50 incoming Members" will join in December, up from 44 now. Its leaders, all Democrats, are Adriano Espaillat (chair), Darren Soto (deputy chair) and Sylvia García (whip) (CHC).
  • CAPAC says at least 22 Asian American and Pacific Islander members are "poised to join" and names five incoming ones (CAPAC).

Republican Governance Group

RG2 is a Republican caucus, and every member named in this section is a Republican unless noted. Its PAC site lists 47 House members. The list is undated (the page was last updated in June 2026) and still includes Kevin Kiley (CA), who has since left the party and ran as an independent. In the 119th Congress the group was chaired by David Valadao, with Carlos Gimenez and Young Kim as vice chairs; Roll Call put its size at 44 in January 2025.

RG2 publishes no endorsements. In the 2026 cycle its PAC (FEC C00433060) gave only to incumbents, leadership PACs and one primary candidate who lost. Nothing ties the group directly to any freshman, so every RG2 tag here is a low-confidence guess. None is evidence of membership, recruitment or contact.

The member list shows two ways in. Some members hold competitive seats (Bacon, Fitzpatrick, Lawler, Valadao). Others are appropriators and committee leaders in safe seats (Womack, Díaz-Balart, Joyce, Calvert, Glenn Thompson); a third of the members hold seats R+10 or redder. A freshman gets the tag only with one of three links to the group: a competitive seat, a predecessor on the list, or a background in appropriations or agriculture. A Freedom Caucus endorsement or a career in movement-conservative institutions rules it out. A Main Street endorsement alone is not enough.

The group took heavy losses in November. Six listed members left their seats (Amodei, Bacon, Carter, Edwards, Letlow, Newhouse), and at least 14 lost: Bresnahan, Ciscomani, Evans, Fitzpatrick, Kean, Kiggans, Lawler, Mackenzie, Miller-Meeks, Steil, Turner, Valadao, Wittman, and Kiley. Two members, Calvert and Kim, ran against each other, and Calvert won. Díaz-Balart, Salazar and Baumgartner held on in close races; Obernolte leads his. On current counts RG2 returns with 25 or 26 members, about half its former size. It will rebuild from those survivors, and this freshman class adds a handful at most.

Known gaps

Biographies are thin for several Republicans who won open seats in Florida and Texas (Rogers, Strada, Gruters, Schwartzel, Bonck) and for one Democrat, Dembo (KY-6).

Margins in uncalled races come from state election offices and the local reporting cited in each row, updated between November 10 and 12. AP has called none of the six. Three are in North Carolina, and all three fall within the state's 1 percent recount margin: Democrat Hubbard leads Republican incumbent Foxx by 335 in NC-5; Democrat Hardy trails Republican incumbent Rouzer by 0.70 points in NC-7; and Democrat Bell trails Republican incumbent Harrigan by 0.84 points in NC-10. Counties canvass on November 13, recount requests are due November 17, and the state certifies November 24.

In Minnesota, Democrat Johnson leads Republican incumbent Finstad by about 300 votes (MN-1); the state certifies on November 19, after which Finstad can ask for a recount. California is still counting mail ballots in CA-4, where Democrat Jones leads Democratic incumbent Thompson, and CA-23, where Democrat Hodge trails Republican incumbent Obernolte.

Three rows show Called before AP has called them, all Democratic pickups: Laubacher over Boebert in CO-4, on CNN's projection; Broderick over DesJarlais in TN-4, on local reports and Wikipedia; and Smith over Murphy in NC-3, on Murphy's concession.
